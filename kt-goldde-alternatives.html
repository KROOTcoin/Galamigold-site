How do you choose an institutional alternative to kt-gold.de?

The single most important criterion is allocated custody paired with transparent spread disclosure. Everything else is secondary. Transparent spread disclosure directly affects portfolio cost at scale, and institutions that focus only on nominal storage fees routinely underestimate realized execution costs on high-volume trades.

Due diligence question template

Custody:

  1. Are positions allocated to specific, serialized bars in your inventory?
  2. Who is the named custodian, and in which jurisdiction are bars held?
  3. Can you provide a bar list or custody certificate on request?

Audits and insurance: 4. Who conducts your third-party vault audit, and how frequently? 5. Can you provide a SOC or ISO attestation covering custody controls? 6. Who is the named insurer, what are the policy limits, and what exclusions apply?

Pricing mechanics: 7. What is your buy/sell spread on a $10M trade? Can you provide historical execution spread data? 8. Are storage and insurance fees itemized separately from trading commissions?

Settlement and chain-of-custody: 9. What is the standard settlement timeline for physical delivery? 10. How is chain-of-custody documented for our internal audit trail?

Reporting and APIs: 11. Do you provide API access or exportable portfolio statements compatible with standard accounting systems?

Legal and jurisdiction: 12. Which regulatory framework governs your custody operations? 13. How does cross-border or bonded storage affect our tax reporting obligations?

SLA and dispute resolution: 14. What are your committed response times for trade queries and custody disputes? 15. Who is our named relationship contact, and what escalation path exists?

Red flags — stop engagement immediately if you see these

  • No third-party vault audit available, or audit is self-certified
  • Positions are unallocated only, with no allocated option
  • Spread quoted verbally with no written confirmation or historical data
  • No named insurer or policy limits provided
  • Jurisdiction of custody is unclear or changes between conversations

Pro Tip: Ask for the named-insurer certificate before signing any mandate. A legitimate institutional custodian will produce it within 48 hours. If the response is a delay or a redirect to marketing materials, treat that as a disqualifying signal. This one step filters out the majority of non-institutional-grade providers.

What custody and regulatory factors matter most to US institutions?

Allocated, LBMA-good-delivery-backed, third-party-audited custody in a recognized jurisdiction is the institutional standard. Everything else is a compromise that carries measurable counterparty or legal risk.

Allocated vs. unallocated: the legal distinction

Allocated accounts grant legal title to specific bars identified in the custodian’s inventory. Unallocated accounts are a creditor claim on a dealer’s pooled inventory and carry materially higher counterparty risk.

Feature Allocated Unallocated
Legal title Investor owns specific bars Creditor claim on dealer pool
Counterparty risk Low (segregated) High (dealer insolvency risk)
Auditability Bar-level verification possible Pool-level only
Typical use case Institutional portfolios, pension funds Retail savings, short-term trading
Insurance Named policy on specific bars Pool-level, often lower limits

Trust signals institutions must require

  • LBMA good-delivery bar handling: confirms bar pedigree and refinery chain of custody
  • Named insurance with stated limits: not a blanket “insured storage” claim, but a certificate with the insurer’s name, policy number, and coverage amount
  • SOC or ISO attestation: covers custody controls, not just physical security
  • Third-party vault audit: independent of the custodian, conducted at least annually
  • Itemized trade confirmations: spread, commission, and storage fee shown separately on every confirmation

Bonded storage and US tax considerations

“Tax-free” storage descriptions often refer to customs-bonded warehouses where VAT is deferred until removal, not eliminated. For US institutional investors, the relevant question is not VAT but custodial jurisdiction: domestic US custody simplifies regulatory reporting, while offshore custody introduces cross-border repatriation considerations and potential withholding complexities. Always confirm with your tax counsel before selecting an offshore vault.

What does institutional onboarding actually look like?

Typical onboarding for a US institutional client takes several business weeks from initial contact to first trade execution, assuming documentation is prepared in advance. Delays almost always trace to incomplete entity documentation or AML review queues.

Onboarding sequence:

  1. NDA and initial inquiry (Day 1–2): Execute a mutual NDA; receive the institutional onboarding pack including custody terms, fee schedules, and audit documentation.
  2. Legal document review (Day 3–7): Review custody agreement, execution terms, and insurance certificates with internal legal counsel.
  3. KYC/AML submission (Day 5–10): Submit entity documents (see list below). AML review runs in parallel with legal review.
  4. Account setup and funding confirmation (Day 10–15): Custody account opened; funding instructions confirmed; execution limits established.
  5. First trade execution (Day 12–20): Test execution at agreed spread; custody confirmation issued with bar serial numbers.
  6. Ongoing reporting setup (Day 15–20): API credentials or statement delivery configured; relationship manager confirmed.

Standard KYC/AML documents for institutional clients:

  • Certificate of incorporation and operating agreement
  • Beneficial ownership register (FinCEN-compliant for US entities)
  • Authorized signatory list with government-issued ID
  • Source-of-funds declaration and AML policy summary
  • Audited financial statements (most recent two years)
  • Regulatory registration or exemption documentation

Sample cost ranges (indicative; always request itemized schedules):

  • Buy/sell spread: typically 0.3%–1.5% depending on volume and provider
  • Storage and insurance: typically 0.1%–0.5% per annum on metal value
  • Commission per trade: varies; some providers embed in spread, others charge separately

Physical delivery requires additional logistics coordination and chain-of-custody documentation. Custody-only settlement is faster and generates a bar-level confirmation that serves as the institutional audit record.
