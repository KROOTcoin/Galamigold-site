What criteria should institutional teams use to compare platforms?

Any serious precious metal trading platforms comparison starts with one gating question: is the metal allocated and titled in your name? If the answer is anything other than a clear yes, the evaluation ends there.

Practitioners confirm that allocated status is the institutional standard of care because unallocated positions carry counterparty risk and lack bailment protections. A vendor that defaults to unallocated bookkeeping is offering you a creditor claim, not a gold position.

Once allocation is confirmed, work through these criteria in order:

Reporting and portfolio integration. Ask whether the platform assigns bar-level or CUSIP-style identifiers, delivers nightly reconciliation files, and produces execution blotters your operations team can import. Integration with legacy wealth management systems is often the primary barrier to institutional gold programs.

Counterparty and custody separation. Verify that the vault is a named third-party commercial depository, not the trading firm’s own facility. Request the vault contract, insurance certificate, and the most recent independent audit or proof-of-reserves report.

U.S. regulatory controls. Confirm OFAC screening, AML/KYC workflows, and tax reporting readiness (Form 1099-B or equivalent). Review the AML compliance framework before signing any mandate.

Operational specifics. Ask for settlement timelines for physical delivery, dispute resolution SLAs, and cybersecurity protocols covering the trading portal and data feeds.

Red flags that should end an evaluation quickly: default unallocated bookkeeping, a fee schedule that lists only a spread with no storage or custody line items, no named third-party vault, and no independent audit within the past 12 months. Any one of these is disqualifying.

Pro Tip: Build a simple scoring rubric before vendor calls. Rate each vendor High/Medium/Low on five axes: allocation model, audit frequency, integration depth, fee transparency, and U.S. compliance controls. A vendor scoring Low on allocation or audit should not advance, regardless of price.

How do custody models actually differ, and why does it matter?

The legal distinction between allocated and unallocated is the most consequential decision in any physical gold program.

An unallocated account makes you an unsecured creditor. The metal sits on the provider’s balance sheet; if the firm fails, you stand in line with other creditors. An allocated account creates a bailment: specific bars, identified by serial number and weight, are legally yours and sit off the provider’s balance sheet entirely.

Segregation goes one step further. Pooled allocated storage means your bars are allocated but stored in a shared vault compartment with other clients’ metal. Segregated-by-client storage means your bars occupy a dedicated compartment, making insurance claims and insolvency recovery cleaner. For foundations, registered investment advisers, and family offices with fiduciary obligations, segregated storage is the defensible choice.

The New York Fed’s gold vault is restricted to central banks and official international organizations. Private institutions must use commercial depositories, which is where custody quality varies most. Recently launched U.S. physically backed bullion ETFs custody 100% of their metal domestically with a named custodian, reflecting the same onshore preference institutional clients should demand.

Audit and chain-of-custody checklist for vendor diligence:

  • Vault contract naming the depository and specifying segregated or pooled terms
  • Bar list with serial numbers, fineness, and weight reconciled to your account statement
  • Insurance certificate covering replacement value, not book value
  • Independent audit or proof-of-reserves report dated within 12 months
  • Transfer receipts for every inbound and outbound movement

Pro Tip: Ask the vendor to provide a redacted sample bar list from a live account. A provider that cannot produce one quickly has a documentation problem worth investigating before you commit capital.

What does institutional execution and settlement actually look like?

Physical gold execution for institutions differs from spot commodity trading. Pricing is typically quoted against the LBMA Gold Price or a live spot reference, with a premium layered on top. Market makers quote two-way prices at set minimum sizes, which is why institutions receive different price mechanics than retail buyers and why execution blotters matter for post-trade verification.

The settlement lifecycle for a physical trade runs through five stages: trade confirmation, payment settlement, physical allocation, vault receipt issuance, and title transfer documentation. Institutions should also review trading gold versus other metals to understand how liquidity and settlement mechanics differ across the precious metals complex.

For reporting, portfolio managers need nightly position files, execution blotters with timestamps and price references, and bar-level identifiers that map to their accounting system. Platforms that expose an API or SFTP feed compatible with systems like Advent Geneva, BlackRock Aladdin, or SS&C Eze eliminate manual reconciliation. Trade management tools that interoperate with institutional platform feeds can further reduce operational friction for ops teams managing multi-asset portfolios.

What fee components should you demand in writing?

Fee opacity is one of the most common problems in physical gold programs. A complete fee schedule covers every line item below.

Fee Component Who Typically Pays Notes
Spot reference premium / spread Buyer Quoted in USD per troy oz above spot
Execution / transaction fee Buyer Flat or basis-point fee per trade
Storage fee Buyer Annual rate on metal value or per-bar fee
Custody / administration fee Buyer Covers account maintenance and reporting
Insurance fee Buyer or included Confirm replacement-value coverage
Independent audit fee Seller or shared Frequency and auditor name matter
Shipping and logistics Buyer (physical delivery) Applies on physical withdrawal
Tax reporting preparation Buyer Form 1099-B or equivalent for U.S. clients

Allocated holdings carry higher custody costs than unallocated balances, and that premium is justified by the removal of counterparty risk. A vendor offering allocated storage at the same price as unallocated is either subsidizing the service or misrepresenting the custody model.

Fee transparency checklist:

  • Published fee schedule with all components itemized, not just the spread
  • Sample execution blotter showing price reference, premium, and total consideration
  • Sample invoice showing storage, custody, and insurance as separate line items
  • Confirmation of whether audit fees are passed through or absorbed

What does onboarding actually take for a U.S. institutional client?

Plan for four to eight weeks from initial inquiry to first settlement, assuming your legal and compliance teams are prepared. Common bottlenecks are beneficial-owner declarations for complex entity structures and sanctions screening for non-U.S. principals.

Required documents typically include entity formation documents, beneficial-owner declarations, proof of accredited investor or qualified purchaser status, OFAC screening results, and a signed custody service agreement. Registered investment advisers should also provide their Form ADV. Preparing a legal template package in advance cuts the negotiation phase by roughly half.

Account minimums vary by institution type and custody model. Segregated storage programs generally carry higher minimums than pooled allocated programs. Confirm the minimum with your vendor early; it affects which custody options are available to you.

Where institutional gold programs go wrong, and how Galamigold addresses it

Three procurement mistakes appear repeatedly in institutional gold programs, and each one is avoidable.

The first is accepting unallocated bookkeeping because the vendor’s documentation never explicitly says “unallocated.” Providers sometimes use language like “gold account” or “metal balance” without specifying title. The fix is simple: require the custody agreement to state “allocated, segregated, client-titled” in writing before any capital moves.

The second mistake is treating audit and insurance as afterthoughts. Teams often request these documents after the mandate is signed, which removes negotiating leverage. Galamigold’s process requires audit and insurance documentation to be presented during onboarding, not after.

The third is underestimating integration complexity. A gold position that cannot be reconciled nightly against your portfolio accounting system creates manual work, audit risk, and valuation delays.
